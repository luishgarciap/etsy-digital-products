## Current observations

### Product families can matter more than a single file

MirthlyStudio presents a digital-stationery model built around coordinated celebration products rather than one isolated invitation. [[20]](../../references.md#ref-20) This supports evaluating **bundle and catalogue economics**, not only the unit price of a single invite.

### Cultural segmentation is observable

JoyablyStudio separates products across Muslim, Sikh, Hindu/Indian, Jewish and other cultural occasions alongside general celebrations. [[19]](../../references.md#ref-19) This is evidence that broad invitation demand can be segmented into more specific buyer contexts.

### Low-price competition is real

DigitalbloomInvite currently lists many editable invitations at heavily discounted low single-digit prices. [[21]](../../references.md#ref-21) A new shop should therefore avoid assuming that a generic invitation can compete simply by being attractive.

### Very large bundles require policy scrutiny

ZenovaVibe publicly offers very large PLR/MRR colouring and education bundles. [[22]](../../references.md#ref-22) Etsy's Creativity Standards require digital products listed as seller-designed to reflect the seller's own creative contribution. [[7]](../../references.md#ref-7) The shop is useful for studying commercial presentation, but its content model should not be adopted without independent rights/policy review.

## Review-mining focus

For each shortlisted opportunity, analyse recent positive and negative reviews for evidence that can change a product decision:

| Theme | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Editing | Canva difficulty, mobile editing, confusing fields |
| Delivery | Download/access problems, instructions |
| Format | Print sizes, phone versions, PDF/PNG needs |
| Personalisation | What buyers expect to edit or customise |
| Bundle | Missing matching products or extras |
| Language / culture | Translation, terminology, cultural fit |
| Quality | Design, pedagogy, print/usability |
| Requests | Products buyers explicitly ask for |

For a serious candidate, the working research sample is approximately **10–15 relevant shops/listings**, deliberately mixing established, mid-sized and newer sellers.

A small number of competitor products can be bought to assess the complete customer journey:

> **purchase → download → instructions → edit → use/export → final experience**

The purpose is customer-experience research, not design copying.
