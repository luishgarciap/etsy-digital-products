## Decision question

> **Which initial digital-product opportunity offers the strongest combination of buyer demand, unmet need, viable economics and repeatable production, and therefore deserves the first controlled pilot?**

## Product directions

### 1. Small-business / café / gastronomy resources

Possible products:

- editable café menus;
- price lists;
- seasonal signs;
- loyalty cards;
- promotions;
- social-media templates.

Potential differentiation to investigate:

- coherent kits for small businesses;
- simple instructions;
- Portuguese, Spanish and English variants where demand exists;
- realistic café/bakery examples;
- products that save real business time.

### 2. Invitations, cards and celebration collections

Possible products:

- birthdays;
- baby showers;
- small weddings;
- thank-you cards;
- Christmas / New Year;
- other special occasions.

Potential differentiation to investigate:

- clear personalisation;
- consistent visual families;
- complete kits such as invitation + card + sign;
- mobile and print versions;
- cultural or bilingual variants where demand exists.

### 3. Bilingual children / family learning

Possible products:

- vocabulary flashcards;
- printable activities;
- learning games;
- visual routines;
- family resources for two languages.

Potential differentiation to investigate:

- educational and linguistic review;
- coherent visual identity;
- Portuguese–English and Spanish–English variants where market evidence supports them.

### 4. Catholic / faith-based products

Current candidate directions include:

- children's faith resources;
- First Communion;
- Confirmation;
- Rosary / prayer resources;
- bilingual faith materials;
- modern faith-based printables.

### 5. Colouring / creative products

Current research now supports comparison of several concrete colouring production models. The remaining question is which buyer/style/product structure could support a coherent and repeatable colouring product family.


## Working terminology

Use these terms consistently so we do not confuse the unit being analysed:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Search term / keyword** | Exact phrase a buyer may type into Etsy search |
| **Seed** | Starting phrase used to discover other searches; not assumed to be attractive |
| **Discovered search** | Search returned from a seed or another research source |
| **Listing** | Individual Etsy sales page |
| **Product** | What the buyer purchases through a listing |
| **Bundle** | One listing containing several related files/items/templates |
| **Product family** | Several separate products serving the same buyer need, theme or occasion |
| **Series** | Use only when the seller explicitly publishes a recurring named sequence or numbered volumes; not a synonym for product family |
| **Shop section** | Category created by the Etsy seller to organise listings inside the shop |
| **Candidate competitor** | Shop worth investigating further |
| **Core competitor** | Shop that repeatedly appears or strongly represents a category |
| **Specialist competitor** | Shop particularly strong in a meaningful sub-niche |
| **Contrast case** | Shop/product useful for understanding a different strategy or what to avoid |

**Rule:** use **product family** by default. Use *series* only when a seller explicitly has a named recurring sequence or volumes.

## Questions for Iteration 01

- **Demand:** what are buyers searching for, for which use cases, and with what seasonality?
- **Supply:** how many relevant listings exist and which offers stand out?
- **Pricing:** what do individual products and bundles sell for?
- **Unmet needs:** what complaints, requests and points of confusion appear in reviews?
- **Differentiation:** where could easier editing, language, niche focus, speed, quality or bundling create value?
- **Economics:** what contribution remains after platform fees, tools, production time and any later advertising?

## Current starting point

All five product directions now have search, competitor/product and review evidence, with different levels of coverage by category. Invitations includes broad-demand and competitor evidence; Café includes measured coffee/matcha searches and three competitor offer models; Bilingual includes measured Spanish searches and both small-resource and large-system competitors; Catholic includes prayer/activity search evidence and concrete buyer-use findings; Colouring includes measured search evidence and three documented production models. No opportunity is prioritised or deprioritised at this stage.
