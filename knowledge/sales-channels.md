# Sales Channels — Etsy, Payhip and Other Options

[Home](../README.md) · [Operations](platform-baseline.md) · [Economics](economics-baseline.md) · [References](references.md)

The following comparison comes from the original 10 November 2026 report for Débora. It is **an option assessment**, not a decision that we must operate more than one shop.

## Etsy versus Payhip

| Aspect | Etsy | Payhip |
|---|---|---|
| Customer acquisition | Established marketplace where buyers can find products by searching | Primarily a storefront supported by our own promotion; a smaller marketplace option |
| Digital delivery | Automatic instant downloads / made-to-order files | Digital delivery |
| Listing and platform fees | US$0.20 listing/renewal, transaction and payment fees; possible set-up charge | Free Forever plan: no monthly charge, unlimited products, 5% platform fee per sale **plus processor costs** |
| Promotion | Etsy Ads, Offsite Ads | Social media, referrals, marketing and available Payhip features |
| Brazil payments | Etsy Payments through Payoneer, with USD payouts | Mercado Pago integration, subject to account/region/currency requirements |
| Overseas audience | Global reach possible but no guaranteed ranking or visibility | Access to international buyers depends mainly on traffic and payment setup |

Source for Payhip plans [[27]](references.md#ref-27); Brazil Payoneer [[4]](references.md#ref-4).

Payhip has a marketplace. The November report noted an application requirement of at least **US$10 in prior sales**, with shop/product review. Reconfirm the policy before relying on this marketplace as a source of new buyers. [[28]](references.md#ref-28)

Payhip's Mercado Pago integration was documented for Brazil. The report flagged typical processing fees from approximately **3.99%**, separate from Payhip's 5%, and Payhip's requirement that the shop currency match the connected Mercado Pago currency. These are **dated observations** that need a test of the actual account, customer currencies and applicable fees. A shop operating in BRL is not identical to charging customers in USD. [[29]](references.md#ref-29)

## Working options

- **Initial option:** Etsy to test demand from buyers already browsing a marketplace.
- **Possible complement:** Payhip if social media, contacts, Pinterest or a future website bring their own traffic and its Brazilian payments setup is practical.
- **Longer term:** Creative Market or Creative Fabrica, only after we have original products, a reason to diversify, and verified acceptance criteria/fees.

A second platform is not required at the beginning. We should decide after testing product demand and the true cost of each channel.

## Questions for practical research

- Who will legally own and receive funds from the Brazilian shop?
- What is actually received in BRL after payment fees, FX, withdrawal costs and relevant taxes?
- Can Payhip accept the intended international orders using a supported payment method?
- Which route attracts buyers at an economically viable total cost?
